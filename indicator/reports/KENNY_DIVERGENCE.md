# Kenny's Divergence Indicator

`pine/Kenny_Divergence_Indicator.pine` (Pine v6) is generated by `scripts/kenny_export_pine.py`
from `reports/cycle2_model_spec.json`. Production HC-MTF is unchanged.

**All evidence is exploratory.** Every candle used had been examined in earlier research, so there
is no independent confirmation. The only unseen data is after the 2026-10-27 freeze, plus the
reserved instruments; neither was read.

## What is on the chart

* **BUY:** a green upward triangle below the candle.
* **SELL:** a red downward triangle above the candle.

Nothing else is drawn by default. State dots and labels, shading and a diagnostics table are
available as optional inputs, all off by default.

Rules for the triangles:
* They fire only on closed candles (`barstate.isconfirmed`).
* Every model input uses closed candles of the chart.
* The higher-timeframe trend (used for optional label tags only) reads the previous completed HTF
  candle.
* A candle with both BUY and SELL draws neither.
* A same-direction repeat within Q = max(2, L/4) candles is suppressed.

## Signal profile "Optimized" (default, `reports/KR_CONFIG.md`, `reports/KR_RESULTS.md`)

The arrows are tuned to be **right enough in retrospect**, not to hit the exact top or bottom. A
signal counts as right when both hold:
* its close is in the lowest 25% (BUY) or highest 25% (SELL) of the surrounding range (±L/2
  candles);
* price has moved the signal's way L/4 candles later.

Per timeframe, chosen on early data and checked on later data (previously studied):

| chart | BUY | SELL | check-period right-rate, BUY / SELL (previous SELL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1W | High Conviction, threshold ×1.1 | exhaustion + reversal near the 52-week high | 0.59 / 0.21 (0.01) |
| 1D | High Conviction | High Conviction top, threshold ×0.9 | 0.54 / 0.30 (0.05) |
| 4H | High Conviction, threshold ×1.1 | Early Warning top, threshold ×0.9 | 0.56 / 0.24 (0.02) |
| 1H and below | High Conviction | Early Warning top | 0.40 / 0.28 (0.19) |

**SELL.** The previous SELL (Confirmed) fired after the top, so it was almost never in the top
quarter of the range. The new SELLs fire earlier: more often near the top, though most are still
not right by this rule.

**BUY.** Mostly unchanged; slightly stricter on 1W and 4H.

**Strong arrows only** (`reports/KS_CONFIG.md`, `reports/KS_RESULTS.md`). Each arrow gets a
strength score: margin beyond its threshold, plus independent evidence (divergence, a price-action
reversal, a volatility/volume burst), plus higher-timeframe agreement. Weak arrows are hidden only
where strong arrows were measurably more often right:
* **1W BUY:** right-rate 0.65 vs 0.59 in the check period, keeping about 80% of arrows. The
  tuning period showed no gain, so the evidence is weak.
* **1H-and-below BUY:** 0.44 vs 0.40, keeping about 45% of arrows.

Everywhere else, every SELL included, strength did not predict being right, so all arrows stay.

**Extra signal types** (`reports/KT_CONFIG.md`, `reports/KT_RESULTS.md`). Four were tested, each
against its own job and a baseline:
* **Shown: breakdown / exit, 4H and below only.** A small red X above the candle means a mature
  uptrend has broken down. It did its job 30–34% of the time, against 17–21% for the baseline.
  It can be switched off with "Breakdown / exit markers".
* **Not shown**, because they were no better than their baseline:
  * caution / take profit (a pullback usually follows any candle near the high anyway);
  * trend-continuation BUY (worse than simply being in an uptrend);
  * bottom forming (early);
  * breakdown on 1W and 1D.

**VIX-weighted BUY** (stocks, ETFs and indices on 1D/1W; `reports/KX_RESULTS.md`). A BUY that comes
within 10 days after a VIX spike-and-reversal is drawn **larger**. Every BUY still shows; only the
size changes.
* **Early data:** such BUYs were right 93% of the time vs 51% (15 arrows).
* **Later data:** only modestly better, 62% vs 59% (21 arrows). It did not pass the full test.
* Turn it off with "Larger BUY after a VIX spike-and-reversal".
* **Timing:** Pine reads the previous completed VIX day, one day more conservative than research.

**Rollback.** Setting "Signal profile = Previous" restores BUY High Conviction / SELL Confirmed
everywhere.

## Internal states and defaults

| state | rule | timing |
|---|---|---|
| Early Warning | score crosses the warning threshold (≈3× the turn rate) | earliest, most false alerts |
| High Conviction | score crosses the high threshold (≈0.75× the turn rate) | before the turn, fewer alerts |
| Confirmed | ≤ L/2 candles after a standard-threshold zone: close beyond EMA21 and a D/4 ATR retreat from the extreme | after the turn |

**Defaults: BUY = High Conviction, SELL = Confirmed.** Both can be changed with the inputs.

**The score per timeframe and direction:**
* **Gradient-boosted trees** (`GBZ`) for **weekly and 4H tops**, the only cells promoted in
  CY-2. In those cells they beat the extension baseline on near-turn rate and net return, in both
  halves of the test years, and in 4/4 robustness variants.
* **ATR extension** (`B_ext`, close − EMA_L in ATR) everywhere else, **including every BUY**.

So **the default BUY is the extension baseline at its high threshold, not a learned model.**

**Turn scale by chart timeframe:**

| chart | turn | L candles |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 1W | major | 26 |
| ≥ 1D | intermediate | 63 |
| ≥ 1H | swing | 4H: 126, 1H: 168 |
| < 1H | local | uses the 1H model; **no research evidence** |

## How the defaults were chosen (frozen benchmark, `reports/KB_CONFIG.md`)

**Data and method.** All candidates ran on the same data, test years, closed-candle timing,
outcomes and costs: 108 instruments, CY-2 walk-forward test years.

**Outcomes measured:**
* win = +D/2 ATR before −D/4 ATR within L/2 candles;
* net = entry at the next open, exit after L/2 candles, after costs.

**Frozen rule result.**
* No candidate materially beat Confirmed in ≥ 5 of 8 cells, so **no combination was adopted**.
* The global default by mean rank was **High Conviction**.

**Checks by role, kept separate for BUY and SELL** (full tables in `reports/KB_RESULTS.md`):

**BUY: High Conviction vs Confirmed and simple baselines.**

| TF | signal | alerts/asset-yr | n | win | net mean | net median | pair-median | lead (candles) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1W | High Conviction | 0.20 | 288 | 0.33 | +4.97% | +2.20% | +4.51% | 2 |
| 1W | Confirmed | 0.34 | 484 | 0.24 | +2.09% | +1.86% | +1.55% | −5 |
| 1W | B_ext std | 0.57 | 813 | 0.29 | +3.96% | +2.16% | +3.38% | 3 |
| 1D | High Conviction | 0.45 | 654 | 0.27 | +2.30% | +1.07% | +1.72% | 7 |
| 1D | Confirmed | 1.16 | 1674 | 0.26 | +2.02% | +0.61% | +1.24% | −5 |
| 1D | B_ext std | 1.34 | 1926 | 0.27 | +2.86% | +1.03% | +2.01% | 7 |
| 4H | High Conviction | 1.96 | 769 | 0.31 | +2.71% | +1.59% | +2.23% | 5 |
| 4H | Confirmed | 5.55 | 2206 | 0.28 | +0.33% | −0.29% | +0.79% | −4 |
| 1H | High Conviction | 7.37 | 2948 | 0.32 | +0.87% | +0.41% | +0.67% | 16 |
| 1H | Confirmed | 17.62 | 7088 | 0.30 | −0.08% | −0.20% | +0.05% | 7 |

**What the BUY checks show:**
* High Conviction has a higher win rate and better median and pair-median returns than Confirmed
  in every cell, so the gain is not a handful of large moves.
* It alerts earlier (before the turn rather than after).
* It gives 2.4–2.9× fewer alerts, but samples stay ≥ 288 trades per cell.
* **Not robust everywhere:**
  * its net gain over Confirmed is negative in the early half at 1D and 4H, and only 1H passes
    the frozen materiality test;
  * at 1D the plain standard-threshold extension baseline has a higher mean net.

**SELL: Confirmed kept.**
* Every SELL candidate **loses money as a short after costs**, on every timeframe.
* High Conviction was worse than Confirmed on net at 1D and 1H and better at 1W and 4H.
* The evidence does not support a change, so the working default (Confirmed) stays.
* Use SELL as a signal to protect or reduce longs, not as a short entry.

**Reference ideas that did not improve Kenny** (as BUY/SELL signals, by the same measures):
* Zone + divergence: modest BUY gains at 1D (net +1.6%, win +0.02), but no consistent pattern.
* Zone + price action: likewise inconsistent.
* Meta-label quality filter: higher SELL win rates at 4H and 1H, but alerts land further from the
  top.
* The standalone references: multi-oscillator divergence (1 or 2 oscillators), hidden divergence,
  price-action reversals, the trend pullback, and the RSI 30/70 baseline.

Most standalone references fire 2–8× more often with much lower precision. Divergence families
reach similar win rates at 4H and 1H only with far more false alerts.

## Verification

* Generated file = generator(committed spec) (test).
* The tree arrays read back from the Pine source reproduce sklearn `decision_function` (to 1e-9,
  including missing values).
* The Python twin of the Pine de-duplication matches the research de-duplication.
* A default-chart test checks for only the two triangle plots.
* `pinewf pine-lint`: only the known false positive (PL001 on `[1]` + `lookahead_on`, the
  completed-HTF-candle idiom).
* `pynescript` parses the script. It is a syntax parser and rejects a deliberately broken copy.
* TradingView limits hit on a phone compile and fixed: CE10205 ("if statement is too long") and
  CE10295 ("main body too long"). Tree data is now compact text inside functions, unpacked once on
  the first candle; a test guards both limits.
* **Not compiled in TradingView.** No TradingView compiler is reachable from this environment,
  so type errors or runtime limits (loop or array limits, request count) are unverified.

## Known limitations

* **Exploratory evidence** on already-examined data. Thresholds were fitted on the research
  universe.
* **Research vs Pine differences** (none measured):
  * The volume features switch on a rolling 50-candle check in Pine, against a whole-history
    check per instrument in research.
  * Research stored CY-2 features as float32; Pine computes in float64. Tree splits very close to
    a threshold could differ.
* The de-duplication was benchmarked with the same state on both sides. With the shipped mixed
  defaults the clash rule interacts slightly differently; this was not measured.
* Charts below 1H, and timeframes between buckets (for example 2H), use the nearest lower model
  and are unvalidated.
* 4H and 1H history covers mostly crypto before 2023 and test years 2021–2026 only.
* **Weekly SELL is a known weak spot.** Three weekly-only follow-up studies tried to improve it:
  * KW-1 and KW-2 (`reports/KW_RESULTS.md`, `reports/KW2_RESULTS.md`);
  * KW-3 (`reports/KW3_DIAG.md`, `reports/KW3_RESULTS.md`).

  The current weekly SELL fires mostly after a decline is under way. About half of the weekly
  SELLs come when price is already ≥ 3 ATR below its 26-week high, and only 7–9% are followed
  by a clean ≥ 3 ATR decline. The best alternative (KW-3 candidate A, exhaustion plus a reversal
  candle or failed new high near the 52-week high) fires near peaks and catches 3–5× more major
  tops. But 45–60% of its signals are followed by further gains in continuing uptrends. It failed
  the frozen adoption rule, so the shipped weekly SELL is unchanged. No tested weekly SELL is
  profitable as a short after costs.

  KW-4 (`reports/KW4_RESULTS.md`) tested three refinements of A, each against one hypothesis for
  its premature signals: stronger exhaustion, failed-breakout confirmation and regime awareness.
  None lowered the premature rate (still about 60% before 2018 and 45% from 2018), so the
  warning-versus-false-alert trade-off is unresolved.

  KW-5 (`reports/KW5_RESULTS.md`) tested a two-state design: an early warning (A), then a weekly
  structure and momentum breakdown within 13 weeks.
  * **Confirmation carries information about the warning.** Confirmed warnings were useful
    28–29% of the time against 4–5% for unconfirmed ones (premature 30–40% vs 73–79%).
  * **Acting on the confirmation itself is no better.** It arrives about 5 weeks later, after a
    2–5% drop, and 14–29% of confirmations are already late. Measured from the confirmation week,
    the useful rate falls to 15–18%, below the warning's own.
  * Neither confirmation-only nor warning + confirmation passed the KW-3 rule, so the weekly SELL
    is unchanged.

  KW-6 (`reports/KW6_SPEC.md`, `reports/KW6_RESULTS.md`) built a full weekly cycle engine, a fast
  vs slow momentum state machine with separate exhaustion and transition stages for both sides.
  It did not merit further testing:
  * **Warnings:** about as useful as simpler warnings (useful rate 0.17–0.18, against 0.19–0.21
    for RSI-70 and Candidate A) but they caught fewer tops.
  * **Confirmations, both sides:** 66–87% were late.
  * **Weekly BUY:** worse than the current weekly BUY in 2018+.
  * **Across all weekly SELL mechanisms tested** (RSI-70, Candidate A, the momentum regime and the
    engine), the useful rate plateaus at about 0.16–0.21.

## How to test in TradingView

1. Pine Editor → paste `pine/Kenny_Divergence_Indicator.pine` → Save → Add to chart. Report any
   compile error verbatim.
2. Try 1W, 1D, 4H and 1H charts. Confirm the chart shows only green ▲ (below the candle) and red ▼
   (above the candle).
3. Repainting check: Bar Replay. Step candle by candle; a triangle should appear only once its
   candle closes and never move or vanish afterwards.
4. Optional: Settings → Advanced display → enable the diagnostics table. It shows the probability
   per side against the warning, standard and high thresholds, the model used, and the research
   evidence.
5. Alerts: "Kenny Div: BUY" / "Kenny Div: SELL", set to "Once per bar close".
